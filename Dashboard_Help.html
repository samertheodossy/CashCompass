Getting started

  1. Open Setup / Review from the top-right.
  2. Add your bank accounts, debts, bills, income, and profile information.
  3. Choose Refresh Financial Plan after your starting information is ready.
  4. Open Planning → This week → Overview for your current priorities.

Upcoming expenses and properties are optional. Add them when they are useful to your household plan.

Setup / Review

Setup / Review shows what is ready and what still needs attention. It does not change your data by itself.

  • Not set up means CashCompass still needs information for that area.
  • Setup complete means enough information is available to use that area.
  • Income-Producing Accounts is optional. Select any investment accounts intended to grow and produce long-term income; CashCompass never guesses from a name or account type. Designation does not override retirement access, tax, liquidity, or withdrawal constraints.
  • Open a card to review its summary and continue to the matching editor.
  • Your profile supplies the name and email used for Financial Plan messages. Date of birth supports retirement calculations.
  • When all required areas are complete, use Go to This week to continue.

Safety: Setup / Review never changes your data on its own. An editor saves only after you choose its Save or Add action.

Overview

Overview summarizes cash, investments, real estate, debt, net worth, financial health, retirement, near-term obligations, and suggested actions.

  • Change vs prior period compares current information with the most relevant earlier Financial Plan refresh.
  • Financial Health explains the main factors helping or hurting the score.
  • Operations Snapshot highlights bills, upcoming expenses, and property spending.
  • Weekly net worth change appears after Financial Plan history is available.
  • Suggested Actions offers practical next steps. Issues keeps the complete warning list separate.
  • Monthly Review stays in the top bar so you can check this month’s banks, houses, investments, and debts. The button is orange while any item still needs this month’s value. It turns green when all four areas are current.

Assets & Liabilities

Bank accounts

  • Update records a confirmed monthly balance for the selected month. It does not change Available Now, Min Buffer, Use policy, or Priority unless you explicitly choose that. Enter $0.00 only when the account is actually at zero. A blank monthly balance is not saved as $0 and does not create evidence.
  • Check Also update Available Now only when you want Planning to use that same amount for bills or debt decisions. The box starts unchecked so a monthly-balance save cannot overwrite a separate cash-availability setting.
  • Min Buffer stays unchanged unless you check that option separately.
  • Add new creates another account. Enter $0.00 only when the opening balance is actually zero. If it is unknown or not available yet, choose Opening balance is unknown / not available yet and leave the amount blank. CashCompass still creates the account, but it does not record a monthly balance, current balance, Available Now, or Min Buffer. A blank opening balance is not saved as $0. Available Now and Min Buffer stay unset unless you explicitly check those boxes, and checking either one changes Planning settings. Manage accounts lists active accounts so you can edit account details or stop tracking them.
  • Edit changes the account name, type, use policy, priority, available amount, and minimum buffer with one Save changes action. Renaming updates the same account across every Bank Accounts year while preserving balances and any import connection.
  • Available Now, Min Buffer, Use policy, and Priority are separate Planning settings. A monthly balance save does not change them unless you select that option. Planning continues to use your CashCompass values.
  • Open Account activity under Manage accounts to review CSV imports or preview a Connected Plaid current balance. CSV and Plaid are separate sources. CSV review, paste, link, and ignore stay on the CSV source. The Connected provider source shows already-mapped bank accounts only and currently applies Current balance only. Preview and staging never change CashCompass balances or Planning settings. Matching and applying require your confirmation. CashCompass values stay authoritative until you confirm.
  • Connected is for institution connection, reload, mapping, reconnect, and disconnect. Import Data opens Account activity, where you can preview provider data and confirm any balance changes. Connected is not the CSV or PDF import surface. Connecting an institution is optional and does not create a Data Readiness action by itself.
  • Stop tracking removes an account from active views while preserving past balances. Show inactive accounts lets you reactivate the existing account later without recreating it.

Investments

  • Update records a confirmed monthly value. Enter $0.00 only when the investment is actually at zero. A blank Update is not saved as $0 and does not overwrite an existing monthly value. Add new creates another account. Manage investments edits identity or stops tracking while preserving history.
  • On Add new, enter $0.00 only when the balance is actually zero. If the balance is unknown or not available yet, choose Balance is unknown / not available yet and leave the starting value blank. CashCompass still creates the account, but it does not record a monthly value, so Monthly Review shows that investment as missing and Planning does not treat it as a verified zero.
  • For accounts marked Income-Producing, open Portfolio activity under Manage. That drawer is the investment import and review surface. CSV and PDF statements are file adapters, not Plaid. Preview first; CashCompass values stay authoritative until you save or apply.
  • The Portfolio view separates imported facts from your intentions: shares, dividends, and the last detected recurring purchase come from saved activity; frequency, amount per occurrence, and paused/active state are editable plans.
  • Use Import CSV to preview a Robinhood export before saving. A newly detected ticker must be included in that income portfolio or marked as not part of this portfolio for now.
  • Where available, statement PDF previews (such as M1, Schwab, Stash, or a retirement savings statement) stay in Portfolio activity. They do not replace Connected provider retrieval.
  • A trusted Schwab or single-account M1 Apply can fill a blank statement month with that statement’s ending account value after you confirm Apply. Preview does not write. If the month already has a value, including an explicit $0, CashCompass leaves it unchanged. Holdings still save separately, and unpriced rows stay excluded.
  • CashCompass remembers the activity reviewed for a ticker that is not currently included, so repeat imports do not ask about the same rows. A later purchase brings the ticker back for review, and previously excluded activity is not silently added if you include it later.
  • Options, fees, and cash administration remain excluded, and repeat imports remain deduplicated.
  • Your recorded investment-account value remains the total CashCompass uses. Imported activity and derived holdings explain the portfolio without silently replacing that account value or silently changing a user-confirmed recurring plan.
  • Connected investments are optional comparison only. Connecting an institution does not create a Data Readiness action by itself and does not change Planning values.
  • Use a clear type such as Brokerage, Retirement, or Education.
  • Stopping tracking preserves past values and removes the account from active totals.

House values

  • Update records a confirmed monthly property value. Add new creates another property. Manage houses edits identity or stops tracking while preserving history. Show inactive houses lets you reactivate the same preserved house later without recreating it.
  • On Add new, house name and property type are required. Current value and remaining loan balance are optional. Enter $0.00 only when the amount is actually zero. Leave a field blank if you do not know it yet; CashCompass does not save a blank amount as $0. A house created without a current value stays in the list, and Monthly Review shows that month as missing until you save a value.
  • Update saves one month’s property value and does not change other months, remaining loan, or expense history. The date must be in this calendar year so a historical valuation cannot make this month look current.
  • A valuation date in this calendar year is saved in that month. A date from another year is kept in Activity and does not fill this year’s tracking. Monthly Review shows the current month as missing until you save a this-year value. If you leave the date blank, this month is used.
  • CashCompass calculates equity from the recorded property value and remaining loan. Unknown amounts are not treated as verified zeros.
  • Each property can also track repairs, fees, insurance, and other expenses.

Debt accounts

  • Open Assets & Liabilities → Debt accounts to add, edit, or stop tracking a debt. Update records a confirmed field value, including an explicit $0.00 Account Balance. That save sets Last Updated for this month so Monthly Review can show Current, even when the amount was already zero. Activity records the save; Monthly Review follows Last Updated, not the Activity row alone. Add new creates the account. Manage debts edits identity or settings, or stops tracking while preserving history.
  • Open Debt activity under Manage debts, or use Connected Import Data, to preview the same Connected Plaid review for already-mapped debt accounts: Current Balance, Credit Limit, Minimum Payment, Due Date, Int Rate, Statement Balance, Statement Date, derived Credit Left, and informational Provider Available Credit when the provider supplies them. Preview does not write. Matching fields show Same and cannot be selected. Missing CashCompass values show Provider information only — not stored in CashCompass. Fields the provider does not send are omitted, not shown as $0. Changed fields apply only after explicit selection and confirmation of the account, field, and value. Credit Left and Provider Available Credit cannot be imported or applied. CSV, OFX, and PDF are not in this drawer yet.
  • Connected is optional. Use it to connect an institution, reload, map accounts, reconnect, and disconnect. Connected manages the provider connection. Import Data opens Debt activity with Connected provider / Plaid selected and keeps the selected institution and mapped account in context. Debt activity is the preview, review, and apply path. Debt activity previews provider data and requires explicit confirmation before CashCompass values change. Preview does not write. Changed fields apply only after explicit field and value confirmation. Connecting an institution does not create a Data Readiness action by itself. Connected is not a CSV or PDF import surface. Planning continues to use CashCompass balances and terms. If provider data has not been imported, no action is required.
  • The debt table lists Account, Type, Balance, Minimum payment, APR, Available credit, and Actions. Available credit for credit cards is Credit Limit minus Balance. Loans and HELOCs show —. A card over its limit shows a warning, Over limit by $X. Provider available-credit figures stay comparison-only and do not overwrite CashCompass calculations. CashCompass does not rewrite stored account values just to display that message.
  • Edit updates the account name, balances, minimum payments, interest rates, limits, and due dates with one Save changes action. CashCompass updates linked Cash Flow references when the name changes. Loans and HELOCs linked to a property can also store verified Principal and Interest and Escrow Payment. Those fields are optional. Blank is unknown, not $0. The full Minimum Payment stays the cash-flow amount. Do not enter an escrow figure whose period is unconfirmed.
  • These source values feed the read-only Debt overview, the actionable Rolling debt payoff, and Net Worth.
  • Stopping tracking preserves history while removing the debt from active totals.

Cash Flow

Cash Flow brings together payments, upcoming expenses, donations, bills, and income.

  • Quick add adds an income or expense amount to the selected payee’s existing total for that month. It does not replace an amount already recorded.
  • Income tracks household income on the existing Cash Flow tab. If this year’s sheet still uses Totals and Yearly Total without Recurring, Frequency, and Expected Amount, Manage income has an explicit action that appends those three blank columns after the last summary column. If those columns already exist, Manage income has a separate Format action that matches their headers to the existing yellow Cash Flow headers, sets readable widths, and applies currency to Expected Amount without adding columns or changing values, formulas, month amounts, totals, or income rows. Add income and Manage → Edit then set Recurring (Yes / No / unknown), Frequency, and Expected amount on the same Cash Flow rows. Each displayed group, such as Cisco Salary or Rent Oakley House, is one forecast even when several history rows exist; those rows are not merged or rewritten. Frequency converts Expected amount per occurrence into a monthly equivalent: Biweekly is 26 times a year (amount × 26 ÷ 12), Semi-monthly / twice monthly is 24 times a year (amount × 24 ÷ 12), and Monthly is 12 times a year. Historical avg is not used as Expected amount. Only Recurring = Yes with a usable frequency and expected amount feeds the Debt Overview extra estimate. Cisco Bonus is two separate annual Cash Flow income rows named Cisco Mid Year Bonus and Cisco End Year Bonus, using Recurring = Yes, Frequency = Yearly, and each row’s own Expected Amount. Current income shows them in an Annual income table; Manage income shows them as standard income rows with Edit and Stop tracking. Mid Year is the first bonus payment of the year; End Year is the second — the actual payment months can change. Manage Income can split an unsplit Cisco Bonus history row into those two named rows after previewing the recorded payment months; the split action is removed after a successful split. Combined month totals stay unchanged. Those amounts are not monthly income, are not used for the 90-day cash reserve, blank stays unknown, and an explicit $0 is a known zero.
  • Bills manages recurring obligations and their schedules.
  • Upcoming keeps larger planned expenses visible before they arrive.
  • Donations has separate Add donation and Manage donations views. Add keeps a read-only Previous donations panel beside the form; Manage lets you safely update every donation field.

Upcoming expenses

  • Add the expense name, due date, amount, payment source, and optional notes.
  • Quick add payment records a payment without retyping the details.
  • Dismiss removes an item from active planning without recording a payment or changing Cash Flow. The Upcoming row and Activity history remain.
  • The 7-day, 30-day, overdue, and total-planned cards update from active items.

Donations

Use Donations as a charitable-giving record for reporting and tax preparation. Choose a tax year, charity, date, amount, and optional comments.

Open Manage donations, then use Edit donation to update the charity, date, amount, tax year, payment type, or comments. When you choose Check, enter the check number in the field that appears. CashCompass verifies that the same row is still present, records the change in Activity history, and restores the prior values if the update cannot be completed safely.

A donation is not automatically treated as a Cash Flow payment. Record the payment separately when you want it included in monthly Cash Flow.

Activity

Activity shows important actions recorded through CashCompass, including payments, bill changes, property expenses, donations, and account updates.

  • Use filters and sorting to find an action.
  • Activity is an audit trail; it is not a financial statement.
  • Correct entry is available for newly recorded direct Quick Add entries. CashCompass first verifies that Cash Flow and any credit-card balance still match the recorded result, then shows every value that will be restored. The newest matching Quick Add can still be corrected when those values match that entry, even if an earlier Manage Debts balance update sits between older payments.
  • A correction preserves the original Activity row and adds a separate correction record. Linked Bill, Upcoming, House, legacy, and changed entries remain read-only until their complete workflows can be corrected safely.
  • Removing an eligible mistaken activity entry does not silently rewrite unrelated financial records.

Bills

  • Due this period shows obligations that still need attention.
  • Add bill creates a recurring bill; Manage bills edits schedules, amounts, sources, and tracking status.
  • Renaming a bill also renames its one exact linked Expense payee on the current year’s Cash Flow. CashCompass refuses missing, ambiguous, or colliding links and rolls back the edit if verification or Activity audit fails.
  • Due Soon and Overdue show obligations that still need attention.
  • Pay opens a side panel where you can confirm the amount and payment date before recording it.
  • After payment, review the Cash Flow total while the six-month payment history finishes loading. Done becomes available when that supplementary history settles, including if it safely times out. The paid occurrence is removed from the due list.
  • Skip asks for confirmation, records no payment, and removes only the selected occurrence. The recurring bill stays active and future occurrences still appear.
  • Stop tracking is available from Manage bills. It removes the recurring bill from active views while preserving its row, schedule details, and Activity history.
  • Autopay identifies bills expected to be handled automatically. Posted expenses use Cash Flow’s red negative-currency format.
  • Weekly and biweekly bills use their configured weekday and schedule anchor when available.

Properties

  • House Expenses records repairs, maintenance, fees, and insurance details.
  • Property performance separates operating income and expenses from actual linked loan payments, then shows after-financing net cash flow.
  • Use consistent property names so income and expenses are grouped correctly.

Property performance

Review each property’s current value, loan balance, equity, rental income, operating expenses, actual linked loan payments, and after-financing net cash flow.

Loan payments use recorded Cash Flow amounts for active Loan or HELOC accounts linked under Assets & Liabilities → Debt accounts. Missing or ambiguous payments stay at $0.00 with guidance rather than being estimated.

Planning

Planning turns your current financial information into decisions. Choose This week, Rolling debt payoff, Debt overview, Retirement, Purchase simulator, or Education from one tool selector. Planning opens on This week by default.

Data refresh and readiness

  • Data status uses four labels: Not connected, Needs attention, Needs review, and Current for required cash and credit-card data. Open Planning → This week → Data for account-level details.
  • Data Readiness checks required monthly cash and credit-card data. Monthly Review checks whether this month’s values are present across active banks, houses, investments, and debts.
  • An empty cash or credit-card area is Not connected. Connected accounts with missing required monthly fields are Needs attention. Malformed or identity-ambiguous evidence without a valid Planning value is Needs review. Valid current monthly Planning values, including an explicit zero, are Current even when imported evidence is stale, conflicting, unmatched, or unavailable.
  • A valid monthly value counts as evidence whether you entered it or obtained it through Plaid, CSV, PDF, or another approved source. Source labels are shown for review; they do not change readiness.
  • Planning, Net Worth, Cash Flow, and debt calculations still use CashCompass INPUT and SYS sheet values. Optional imported records are listed separately under Optional provider information and do not fail readiness when Planning already has a valid current monthly value.
  • Action buttons appear only when a required monthly CashCompass value is missing. On Data Readiness those actions are Open Bank Accounts or Open Debts for that account and month. Missing monthly values for banks, houses, investments, and debts are handled in Monthly Review. Current items have no action button and say Current — no action required. Opening an editor does not save, match, apply, or mark the item reviewed; explicit confirmation is still required.
  • Optional provider information is reference-only when a valid CashCompass value already exists. Connecting an institution or retrieving provider data does not create a Data Readiness action by itself. It does not require Plaid, does not create an action item, does not open Connected Accounts, and does not replace the existing CashCompass value. Connected is not a CSV or PDF import surface. Connected Bank Accounts lists only institutions that are actually connected.
  • If a card has multiple possible imported rates, Cash Compass does not guess. A valid sheet APR still counts as evidence. You may verify the APR that applies to the carried balance through the preview-and-confirm review flow.
  • Investments, properties, and retirement are maintained in CashCompass but are not yet included in this readiness review. Existing CashCompass values remain authoritative.
  • This Week is the primary read-only plan that reconciles current available cash across required payments, the 90-day operating floor, Samer Robinhood’s normal $500 weekly policy, account-buffer restoration, highest-APR debt, and money that should wait.
  • Debt Overview is a read-only comparison of current balances, minimums, and payoff outlook, including a hypothetical avalanche vs snowball extra-payment scenario that is not saved and does not make a payment.
  • Rolling Debt Payoff turns those current balances into a month-by-month action plan.
  • To change a balance, rate, minimum, due date, tracking status, or optional Loan/HELOC property link, use Assets & Liabilities → Debt accounts.
  • Retirement evaluates your selected assumptions and scenarios. Current investable assets exclude accounts typed Education or 529; other investment types stay in the total.
  • Purchase Simulator explores affordability without changing saved account balances.
  • Education saves school, cost, and contribution choices for the exact accounts Lutfi 529 and Laith 529. Current balances come from Investments and are not copied. Blank amounts mean unknown; enter $0.00 only when the amount is actually zero. Save and Clear write only Education inputs. Forecasts are not calculated yet.

Education

Use Planning → Education to record what you know about each 529 plan. This view does not change Investments, Bills, Cash Flow, or holdings.

  • Each saved plan stays on its own exact account: Lutfi 529 and Laith 529. The two plans do not mix.
  • You can save school or program stages, semester tuition, monthly living costs, one-time costs, annual estimates, current contribution baselines, planned Continue / Reduce / Pause choices, dates, overlap and remainder policies, scenario rates, and Estimate or Confirmed status.
  • Leave a field blank when it is unknown. An explicit zero is allowed only when you enter it.
  • Forecasts and coverage calculations are not part of this screen yet.

Advanced planning

Use these deeper views after your everyday accounts, bills, and income are current.

This Week

This Week shows what CashCompass can recommend from current recorded information. It does not transfer money, pay bills, change balances, place trades, or mark an action completed.

  • Your plan this week leads with three customer actions: how much to invest, how much credit-card debt to pay, and how much cash to keep available. Expand If you follow this plan to see recommended payoffs, recommended extra payments, required payments, expected balances, and projected first-year interest effects. CashCompass uses Paid off, Payment completed, and Confirmed balance only after refreshed authoritative data confirms the result. Calculation details retain the deeper reasoning and audit trail.
  • The default Overview is the daily cockpit: this week’s actions, compact progress, Plan notes for allocation warnings and recommendations, and the next step. A stronger action label is used only when you must correct missing or invalid data. Use Debt, Cash, Investments, Properties, and Forecast for the detailed inputs, outcomes, and calculations behind each area.
  • Planning warnings are not the same as missing monthly data. Review monthly completeness in Monthly Review and cash/card readiness in Planning → Data.
  • Robinhood policy floor is the user-defined $500 weekly base contribution. It is shown separately from bills and optional acceleration. A higher APR alone never pauses it; an override is allowed only when required bills or debt minimums cannot be covered, projected cash would be negative, the hard operating floor would be breached, or an explicitly configured emergency is active. CashCompass names the exact constraint.
  • Protected cash remains outside deployable cash through your account policies and minimum buffers.
  • Do Not Touch is an intentional Use Policy. Those accounts stay visible but are excluded from available household cash. This Week does not treat that policy as missing data, does not create an action item for it, and does not require you to change it. Children’s accounts remain protected.
  • 90-day operating reserve protects recorded future Bills, debt minimums, Upcoming expenses, and an allowance for irregular property costs. Normal future Robinhood policy contributions remain visible in the forecast but do not inflate the solvency floor. Estimated recurring income reduces the forecast, but future income is never treated as cash available today.
  • Capital Deployment Pace separates how much to deploy from where it should go. The hard operating floor remains non-negotiable. The default Balanced preference adds an auditable cushion equal to the largest of one normalized month of forecast operating outflows, current monthly debt service, or one normalized month of property contingency, plus any configured income-stability, cash-flow-volatility, or known-event cushion. Only cash above that preferred target enters the existing optimizer.
  • Weekly proposal identity is stable for unchanged facts throughout the same Monday-based planning week. Confirmed and awaiting-confirmation deployment amounts reduce the remaining period capacity so refreshing does not create another copy of the same budget. Until authoritative period tracking is connected, the screen labels the result as a snapshot-only proposal.
  • Compare deployment pace shows Faster payoff, Recommended, and More liquidity scenarios. Cash account yields are not yet authoritative, so source economics remain labeled CASH_YIELD_DATA_REQUIRED.
  • Variable Bills use their saved estimates. Episodic healthcare expenses may use $0 when nothing is scheduled; add an Upcoming expense or saved estimate when a visit or bill is expected.
  • Recurring gross rent is the property-income input. Mortgages and scheduled HOA, management, insurance, utilities, tax, cleaning, and warranty costs come from Bills, debt minimums, or Upcoming expenses exactly once. House history adds only a trailing allowance for irregular Repair, Maintenance, Appliance, and Other costs, reduced by matching planned Upcoming costs.
  • Planned repairs reduce overlapping historical contingency, but they cannot erase it. CashCompass keeps an unknown-repair floor equal to 25% of the trailing 90-day irregular-property allowance.
  • Existing investment contributions such as M1, Robinhood, or Stash are shown separately for review. Stopping future M1 funding, transferring M1 securities in kind, and selling/redeploying M1 positions are three different decisions. Taxable sales remain blocked as TAX_DATA_REQUIRED until position and tax-lot inputs exist.
  • Eligible Cash Sources shows each current account’s balance, Use Policy, minimum protected buffer, amount eligible now, Planning Role, and inclusion status. An eligible positive-balance account with a $0 buffer is prominently flagged because CashCompass otherwise treats its full balance as deployable.
  • Other capital decisions keeps expected future free cash flow, optional contribution redirects, in-kind transfer reviews, and taxable sale reviews separate from cash available today.
  • Each brokerage source shows whether a separate stable Investment Id verifies its identity. Retirement and custodial assets such as a 401(k) are retained for household analysis but excluded from actionable funding-source candidates.
  • Recommendation state keeps the plan honest: Proposed uses recommended, expected, and projected wording; Awaiting confirmation means a payment was reported but refreshed authoritative data has not confirmed its result; and only Confirmed may use completed, paid off, or confirmed-balance wording. Projected released minimums and the conditional next plan remain unavailable until refreshed balances show the action occurred. Superseded is reserved for replaced recommendations.
  • What happens next shows what will be reranked after confirmation—remaining revolving debt, Robinhood acceleration, economically competitive loans, and additional liquidity—without treating proposed payments or released minimums as completed facts.
  • Why not the alternative? compares the recommended action with a competing use, such as paying guaranteed high APR versus an uncertain investment return.
  • Plan at a glance groups bills and commitments, reserve protection, account-buffer restoration, highest-APR extra debt, investment funding, and the preferred liquidity intentionally retained.
  • After required minimums, reserve protection, and the Robinhood policy floor, every positive revolving balance enters a debt avalanche from highest APR to lowest. There is no 20%, 15%, 10%, or other payoff cutoff. Loans remain visible and are compared generically using effective cost, tax treatment when known, penalties, liquidity, and the uncertain investment-return alternative.
  • The expandable calculation details disclose the expected investment-return range and base assumption used for loan comparisons. These are configurable planning assumptions, not guaranteed returns or debt-class cutoffs.
  • What waits keeps zero-dollar choices visible without mixing them into this week’s actions.
  • View calculation details expands the complete ordered ledger and every eligible choice when you want to audit the arithmetic.
  • If important information is missing or cannot be reconciled, the plan pauses allocation and explains what needs review.
  • The monthly card currently summarizes the same current-week decisions; it does not invent future pay dates or income.

Debt overview

Debt Overview compares balances, required payments, payoff timing, and interest impact. It is a planning view, not a promise of future results.

Debt Overview shows this month’s extra-payoff recommendation first. Each month it uses the supported extra estimate to recommend an extra payment and a payoff strategy from current saved facts. Account totals, the payoff chart, and the debt table stay in a collapsed Debt balances and payoff estimates section. Property-loan payoff months in that summary use principal-and-interest only at the saved APR; the full minimum stays the budgeted payment and is not applied to principal. If P&I versus escrow is not saved, that row’s estimate is incomplete rather than using the full minimum. An optional hypothetical overlay still compares minimum payments only, avalanche (highest APR first), and snowball (smallest balance first) if you type a different amount or include a Cisco bonus.

  • The comparison screen stays compact: this month’s extra-payoff recommendation is at the top, immediately after the Debt Overview heading, a compact Refresh control beside that heading, and related links (the amount, including $0 extra this month when none is available; the monthly $0 explanation in Why extra stays $0.00; and a compact three-month cash-safety summary of projected cash, protected reserve, and the amount above reserve at month 3). Four visually distinct sections keep related information together: This month’s action and Monthly cash picture; Household payoff strategy comparison; Debt balances and payoff estimates; and Calculation details and source data. Expanded details stay inside their own section instead of running into the next one. A compact Monthly cash picture stacks four cards: income, household bills, debt payments, and investment commitments. Each card shows its total and a brief breakdown when collapsed, and a specific disclosure such as View income sources or View debt accounts stays inside that card. Opening a card expands its saved sources downward inside the same border; other cards do not reflow around it, and only one Monthly cash picture card can be open at a time. Amount column headings sit above their amounts. The monthly shortfall is a separate red or rose summary below those four cards and has no disclosure control; that shortfall amount is shown once rather than repeated in supporting text. If a source group has no saved rows, its disclosure still opens and says those sources are not listed, rather than inventing amounts. Repeated shortfall explanations stay collapsed. A separate one-time extra from cash above reserve follows that monthly result with a brief explanation that does not repeat the usable-cash-below-reserve warning; it is not added to the recurring extra and is not part of the optional hypothetical scenario. The month-3 amount is labeled “Above reserve at month 3 — not safe to spend”; it is informational context and is not available to spend. Account totals, the payoff chart, and the debt table stay in a collapsed Debt balances and payoff estimates section. “How this was calculated” stays collapsed inside Calculation details and source data. When the Monthly cash picture is shown, it does not repeat the same income, bill, and debt itemization; Upcoming rows used in the cash-safety check stay there. The household payoff comparison stays visible as a table that keeps amounts, dates, and labels together. When extra is not affordable, the modeled lowest-interest result is labeled Lowest modeled interest and Conditional lowest-interest scenario and is not affordable or recommended to act on this month; the other two strategies stay in collapsed Other modeled strategies. Per-debt payment releases and detailed payoff orders start collapsed, and opening a strategy’s details renders that panel across the full comparison width beneath that row. Deficit amounts use red or rose, hypothetical and conditional results use amber, and informational totals stay neutral or blue; hypothetical savings are not colored green. When extra can be proven, liquidity and estimated payoff or growth appear once; the other uses stay in collapsed details. A separate optional hypothetical scenario stays collapsed for a typed extra or Cisco bonus. Missing or invalid facts stay visible as unknown data and are not replaced with $0. Native disclosure controls stay keyboard-accessible. On a narrow screen, each comparison strategy stacks as its own block.
  • The monthly recommendation uses the supported extra automatically. You do not type an amount for that recommendation. Refreshing Debt Overview recomputes it from current saved facts; it does not reuse a stored snapshot. The recommendation can appear before the payoff chart and table finish loading. Keep monthly bank, house, investment, and debt values current in Monthly Review; that review is not duplicated here.
  • A household payoff plan on this same page compares all saved active debts, including credit cards, a car loan, HELOC, and property loans, using saved balances, APRs, Types, and minimums. It shows total interest, the debt-free date, and when a contractual payment is released. Keeping a paid-off debt’s payment improves monthly cash flow; rolling that payment to the next debt can finish sooner but does not improve cash flow until the rollover stops. Those are not the same benefit. Mortgage amortization uses principal-and-interest only; taxes, insurance, and escrow stay in household cash flow after payoff and are not applied to principal or counted as freed debt payments. The monthly extra estimate counts a property loan’s full saved minimum, which may include escrow, and does not add property-tax or homeowners-insurance bills that are saved as linked to that mortgaged property again. Property-tax and insurance bills for properties with no linked mortgage stay in the bill total and do not require an escrow match. Extra is withheld only when a bill is saved as linked to a mortgaged property and that mortgage’s escrow coverage is unknown, rather than guessed from the bill name. Rolling debt payoff

This view estimates how much cash can be used after protected balances, buffers, upcoming expenses, and other near-term risks.

  • Safe to use is the maximum amount available after protections.
  • Cash to use now is your chosen amount, capped at the safe limit.
  • Standard payoff follows the normal priority model.
  • Aggressive payoff explores faster allocation while keeping the same safety cap.
  • Show details explains assumptions and forecasts for users who want a deeper review.
